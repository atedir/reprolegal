Single Fathers and Surrogacy: Destinations to Consider in 2026

A practical guide for single men exploring surrogacy in 2026, covering which destinations are most accessible and what to expect legally.

— · 4 September 2026

The Landscape for Single Men Pursuing Surrogacy

The number of single men building families through surrogacy has grown steadily over the past decade, yet the legal and clinical landscape remains uneven. Many countries that accept heterosexual couples — or even same-sex couples — impose additional restrictions on single intended fathers, or exclude them outright. Understanding which destinations are genuinely accessible, and what conditions apply, is the essential first step before committing any time or resource to a programme.

This article surveys the key jurisdictions most commonly considered as of 2026, outlines the legal posture of each, and explains the practical factors that typically affect eligibility. It does not constitute legal or medical advice; a note to that effect appears at the foot of the article.

Why Destination Choice Matters More for Single Fathers

For a single intended father, destination is not simply a question of cost or convenience. It is, in most cases, the determining factor in whether a programme is possible at all. Several variables interact:

  • Eligibility criteria in local surrogacy law. Some statutes specify "a couple" as the only permissible category of intended parent, which excludes single applicants by definition.
  • Parental order or court order routes. In jurisdictions where parentage must be established through post-birth court proceedings, the legal test for single parents may differ from that for couples.
  • Egg donation requirements. A single man requires both a surrogate and an egg donor. Not every destination permits egg donation alongside surrogacy, or permits anonymous donation, which affects programme design and cost.
  • Exit documentation for the child. Even where a destination accepts a single father, the intended father's home country must be willing to issue a passport or travel document to the child, and to recognise his parentage. This is a separate legal question that must be resolved in advance.

Destinations That Are Generally Accessible in 2026

Georgia

Georgia has been one of the more accessible destinations for international intended parents for a number of years. As of writing, Georgian law permits altruistic surrogacy and requires the intended parent or parents to be genetically related to the child. Single men are not explicitly excluded under the current legislative framework, though practice can vary between clinics and legal practitioners, and the situation warrants verification with local counsel before proceeding.

Egg donation is permitted and widely available. The intended father is typically registered as the legal parent at birth, which simplifies exit documentation. Georgia is a relatively cost-effective destination; typical all-inclusive programme costs (excluding travel and accommodation) fall within a broad range and should be discussed directly with coordinating agencies, as they vary considerably depending on clinic selection and medical complexity.

Colombia

Colombia operates under a civil-law framework that does not have a specific surrogacy statute as of writing. Surrogacy arrangements have been conducted under contractual and constitutional principles, with parentage established through court proceedings. Single fathers have completed programmes in Colombia, and the legal environment has generally been regarded as relatively permissive, though the absence of a dedicated statute introduces uncertainty. Engaging experienced Colombian legal counsel before signing any agreement is strongly advisable.

Egg donation is available. The timeline for obtaining a court order confirming parentage can vary and should be factored into planning. Colombia's geographic accessibility from both North America and Europe makes it a practical consideration for many intended parents.

Mexico (selected states)

Surrogacy regulation in Mexico is determined at the state level. As of writing, a small number of states permit surrogacy for international intended parents, including single men, while others have introduced restrictions or moratoriums. The states most commonly used for international programmes have generally permitted single intended fathers to proceed, but the regulatory position has shifted in recent years and continues to evolve. Legal advice specific to the relevant state is essential, as is confirming the current position with your coordinating agency at the outset.

Canada

Canada permits altruistic surrogacy and does not restrict intended parenthood on the basis of marital status or family structure. Single men may pursue surrogacy in Canada. The key constraint is that Canadian law prohibits commercial surrogacy; surrogates may be reimbursed for eligible expenses only, which affects the pool of available surrogates and can extend matching timelines considerably.

Parentage laws vary by province, and a pre-birth order confirming the intended father's legal parenthood can generally be obtained in several provinces, though the process and timing differ. Canada is a popular choice for intended parents based in the United Kingdom, the United States, and Australia, partly because post-birth recognition of Canadian surrogacy arrangements in those countries tends to be more straightforward than arrangements made in commercial surrogacy destinations.

The United States

The United States remains one of the most legally secure destinations for surrogacy, with several states offering pre-birth parentage orders that name the intended father as the sole legal parent from birth. Single men are accepted by agencies and clinics in surrogacy-friendly states without restriction as a matter of general practice.

The significant drawback is cost. US surrogacy programmes are among the most expensive globally, with total costs typically spanning a wide range depending on state, clinic, surrogate compensation, and legal fees. For intended parents for whom legal certainty is the overriding priority, the United States warrants serious consideration despite the financial commitment. Destinations That Are Generally Inaccessible to Single Fathers

A number of jurisdictions that operate surrogacy programmes restrict eligibility to married heterosexual couples. These include several countries in Eastern Europe and parts of Asia where legislation explicitly defines intended parents as a husband and wife. Single men, irrespective of other circumstances, are excluded under those frameworks as of writing.

It is worth noting that legal positions change. A destination that is inaccessible in 2026 may introduce legislative reform in subsequent years, and the reverse is equally true: a currently accessible destination may tighten its framework. Any programme that extends over twelve months or more should include a monitoring step to confirm that the legal basis for the arrangement remains sound throughout.

Practical Considerations Specific to Single Fathers

Parental responsibility in your home country

Before selecting a destination, you should obtain a clear opinion from a family law solicitor or attorney in your country of residence on how a surrogacy arrangement in that destination will be treated at home. Some countries will recognise the intended father's legal parenthood by operation of law or straightforward registration; others require a court application or parental order. The process can take months and, in some cases, longer.

Egg donor matching

As a single father you will need to select an egg donor as well as match with a surrogate. These two processes may run concurrently or sequentially depending on the destination and the agency's protocol. Ensuring the agency you work with has a sufficient and well-screened donor pool is part of the due diligence process. Our programmes overview explains how reputable agencies structure these parallel processes.

Support structures

Practical planning for life after the birth — including parental leave, childcare, and the logistics of returning home with a newborn — is not a peripheral concern. It is worth addressing these questions early, particularly when the destination requires an extended post-birth stay before exit documentation is issued.

Conclusion

Single fatherhood through surrogacy is achievable in 2026, but it requires careful and early attention to destination selection, home-country recognition, and the specific eligibility requirements of each jurisdiction under consideration. No destination is universally straightforward, and the legal landscape continues to shift. Working with an experienced coordinating agency and engaging independent legal counsel in both the destination country and your country of residence remains the most reliable way to navigate the process.
